Overview

Abstract

Retargeting human demonstrations into dynamically feasible dexterous hand trajectories requires resolving complex contact physics, yet current methods either omit physical constraints or rely on computationally expensive sampling-based optimization in simulation. Reinforcement learning has previously been used to achieve planning and control through contact dynamics; however, standard policies require object-specific training and fail to generalize across arbitrary geometries. This work reframes physics-based retargeting as a residual learning problem, where we demonstrate that zero-shot generalization across objects relies on structured state representations. Specifically, formulating the observation space to include a localized contact-error signal guiding each robot link to its target coordinate on the object surface enables a single, unified residual policy to successfully grasp novel, unseen object geometries. Rolling out this policy in simulation on a kinematic reference trajectory yields a physics-based retargeter, OATH. Operating at 0.4 seconds of compute per second of reference, OATH achieves 2.4× faster-than-real-time performance and a 60–350× reduction in computational cost compared to sampling-based methods. The system recovers 58% of held-out and 54% of unseen-object DexYCB trajectories while maintaining a sub-2 cm tracking error.

Method

Reference generation

Hover over a stage to see what it does.

1 · MANO trajectory

MANO meshes are fit overtop of a kinematic trajectory extracted from video or given by the DexYCB dataset.

Human demonstration videos provide high-level kinematic guidance, but direct joint-space mapping alters contact locations due to morphological disparities between human and robot end-effectors.

2 · TopoRetarget

Morphological discrepancies between human MANO meshes and the target robotic hand prevent direct joint mapping. We utilize TopoRetarget [5] to map human mesh surface interactions onto the robot hand mesh while maintaining functional contact topology.

3 · Contact extraction

Formulating contact-error signals for policy training requires reference contact locations and contact presence labels. Contacts are recorded for the 16 hand bodies with collidable geometry.

4 · Inverse kinematics

Continuous tracking of target wrist poses requires coordinated motion across an arm-hand system. We apply differential inverse kinematics over the arm kinematic chain to align the arm with the wrist.

5 · TOPP

Kinematic retargeting and IK produce trajectories that may violate physical motor limits. We apply Time-Optimal Path Parameterization (TOPP) [6] to adjust trajectory timing subject to joint velocity, acceleration, and torque constraints.
