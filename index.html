Overview

Abstract

Retargeting human demonstrations into dynamically feasible dexterous hand trajectories requires resolving complex contact physics, yet current methods either omit physical constraints or rely on computationally expensive sampling-based optimization. We reframe physics-based retargeting as residual learning, and show that zero-shot generalization across objects comes from the observation space: a localized contact-error signal that guides each robot link to its target on the object surface lets a single residual policy grasp novel object geometries. Rolling this policy out in simulation on a kinematic reference yields a physics-based retargeter, OATH, that runs 2.4× faster than real time.
